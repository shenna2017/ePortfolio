## Overview

This ePortfolio documents the design, implementation, and evaluation of an inclusive AI learning environment developed across one full academic year (September 2025 – April 2026). It evidences the transformation of an AI module delivered at ATU Galway City through the systematic application of Universal Design for Learning (UDL) principles, grounded in constructivist and socio-constructivist educational theory.

The portfolio is structured across six sections corresponding directly to the EDUC09043 assessment criteria. Each section contains collapsible reference panels in Harvard citation style. Navigation is available through the fixed top navigation bar or by scrolling continuously through the page.

## Portfolio Structure

### Section 01 · Teaching Philosophy
**Assessment criterion (i):** Teaching philosophy, providing a description of the educator's view as to the underlying principles upon which approaches to inclusive practice are based, with reference to conceptual frameworks and theoretical perspectives.

- Core Philosophy — four numbered subsections covering philosophy foundations, theoretical grounding, design and practice, and inclusivity as principle
- Theoretical Foundations — eight key theorists presented as expandable cards (Bransford et al., Kolb, CAST, Mayer, Nicol and Macfarlane-Dick, Prince, Novak, Rose and Meyer)
- Nine Principles of Inclusive AI Education — research-informed principles operationalised through the AI Learning Dashboard
- Kolb's Experiential Learning Cycle — applied to the four-stage AI module structure
- Assessment and Feedback — four accordion entries on structured reflection, progress visibility, iterative design, and flexible demonstration
- Well-being and Reflection — continuous reflection, growth mindset, and conclusion
- **References** · collapsible panel · 10 Harvard-formatted entries · located at the end of the Well-being and Reflection tab

### Section 02 · Student Voice and Reflective Evidence
**Assessment criterion (ii):** Student reflections, showcasing student reflections evidenced.

- Pre-intervention authenticated student reflections identifying four structural gaps in the existing AI module
- Four student-named changes mapped to post-intervention outcomes (Section 05)
- Literature grounding panel — Boud, Keogh and Walker (1985); Moon (2004) — framing reflections as evidence of metacognitive engagement
- Thematic analysis of student-named changes with post-survey confirmation references
- **References** · collapsible panel · 15 Harvard-formatted entries · located at the bottom of Section 02

### Section 03 · UDL Plan
**Assessment criterion (iii):** Module UDL plan, rationale and description for change, adaptations and accessibility plan, with sufficient context describing the rationale for change undertaken and description of change undertaken.

- CAST UDL Guidelines 3.0 audit results — 21 accessibility barriers identified across the existing module
- Three-column UDL framework mapping (Representation, Engagement, Action and Expression)
- Tiered module architecture — AI Foundations, Deep Learning, Advanced Concepts (6 modules per tier, 18 total)
- Accessibility architecture — multi-language selector, dyslexia font toggle, Web Speech API read-aloud, WCAG 2.1 AA compliance
- Rationale for each change mapped to student-identified gaps from Section 02
- **References** · collapsible panel · Harvard-formatted entries · located at the bottom of Section 03

---

### Section 04 · Implementation of Change
**Assessment criterion (iv):** Implementation of change, evidence of change undertaken in relation to UDL implementation, with thorough explanation and evidence of suggested changes made within the module in relation to UDL.

- Module-by-module implementation evidence across all 18 modules and 3 tiers
- Representative module walkthroughs — A* Algorithm (AI Foundations), CNN (Deep Learning), Diffusion Models (Advanced Concepts)
- GAN evidence panel — simulation-first pedagogy with student engagement data
- Figure 3 — per-student localStorage reflection system architecture
- Figure 4 — CAST UDL 3.0 mapping applied to the dashboard
- Accessibility panel implementation evidence — font scaling, dyslexia font, read-aloud, language selector
- **References** · collapsible panel · Harvard-formatted entries · located at the bottom of Section 04

---

### Section 05 · Evaluation and Effectiveness
**Assessment criterion (v):** Evaluation and effectiveness of change, including student feedback from, and responses to, student surveys.

- Post-intervention survey results (MSc cohort, n=15) — five Likert-scale domains
- Survey mean score: 4.19/5 across all domains
- Completion rate improvement: 65% → 94% (+29 percentage points)
- Post-intervention student reflections — seven named student entries (A.M., P.R., T.N., M.L., Ch.D., J.K., and others)
- CIPP evaluation framework (Stufflebeam) — Context, Input, Process, Product analysis
- Engagement data: 186 authenticated reflections submitted February–March 2026
- Accessibility data: 97% completion rate for students activating at least one accessibility feature
- **References** · collapsible panel · Harvard-formatted entries · located at the bottom of Section 05
